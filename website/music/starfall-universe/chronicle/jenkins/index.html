Miss Jenkins

When the walls answer

One of the original Prefects, Miss Jenkins brings centuries of experience to fleet command and a hostage rescue.

Older than the institution

Miss Jenkins enters Starfall as one of the original Prefects and as a woman older than the founding of the Prefects themselves. Centuries of service are part of her authority. Stories place her farther back still, perhaps before stellar expansion from the origin worlds, but that remains rumor. Her exact age, beginning and final senior service title have not been fixed. An impressive legend is no reason to print an unearned rank.

Her visual identity is distinctive: a refined adult woman with a humanlike face, feline ears, vivid green eyes and silver-white hair. Black, white, gold and emerald tailoring, gloves, immaculate composure and a knowing expression belong to her. She is not an elderly wizard, a generic shipboard computer or a full-furred snow leopard in a different coat. The manners are exact, the wit dry, and the warmth compatible with a very sharp edge.

The intelligence behind apparent foresight

Fleet command is her primary role. Master ship intelligences link through her; implants, sensor angles, stealth drones and multiple coordinated perspectives give her a remarkably complete operational picture. She can place an order where the rest of the team will need it a moment later. The effect can look like prophecy from the corridor below. The author’s present explanation is sensor fusion, knowledge, preparation and formidable multitasking. Actual temporal powers have not been established.

This gives Starfall a new scale of competence without making the other Prefects redundant. Inara still reads the local geometry. Lenthrea still puts herself where the breach or protected person requires her. Marines still move, hold and recover. Jenkins links those partial views into an action that feels impossibly clean because the work of seeing it was already under way.

The hostage rescue

The original siege premise puts the danger inside a hostage station: welded concourse doors, chained civilians beneath departure boards and service passages older than the criminals occupying them. Jenkins can read the building as well as the network. Marine feeds and multiple Prefect teams make the walls into a coordinated problem rather than an excuse to level the station. The later, tighter rescue revision supplies the decisive duck-and-fire exchange and governs that moment of the story.

The Walls Answer introduces that coordination through a condensed siege and hostage rescue. The commander obeys Jenkins’s order to duck. Lenthrea, concealed behind him, fires at the instant the opening becomes real. What looks from one position like a wall, a blind spot or an impossible timing problem is already part of a larger firing solution. Jenkins guides Starfall, War-Tigress and the marines through an operation whose authority feels quietly overwhelming.

The ending gives her the right scale again: a little girl’s hand, taken elegantly and gently as Jenkins leads her out of the secured site. The image connects the founder’s immense service with the reason the institution should exist. It is not enough that the walls answer her. The person inside them must get to leave.
