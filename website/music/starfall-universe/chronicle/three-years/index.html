The Three Years

Three years. Three days. Three days.

The figures repeat because each measures a different kind of waiting: a world abandoned, justice finally moving, and a young woman preparing to leave.

When refusal became a war

Corporate pressure first arrived as contracts, development proposals, legal contests and the familiar accusation that a living world was being wasted by the people who loved it. When Tal’Shyra would not yield its forests, river systems, snow country and commons, deniable mercenary bands and pirate contractors began making resistance expensive. Raids, sabotage, assassinations and terror were meant to turn local refusal into exhaustion.

Predator and prey-descended communities held together. Northern snow-leopard coalitions mastered stealth and terrain. Southern ursine holds sheltered logistics and defense. Wolves and lions of the central plains coordinated supply and movement. Builders, guides, agritech engineers, healers and households became the guardian militias. Their tools and tactics changed under pressure. Their lack of an appetite for conquest had never meant an inability to defend a village.

The daughter in the war

Inara grew into danger while still young. She fought beside Zyara, using the old leopard disciplines where mercenary armor left seams and blind angles. Kael’Rath was nearly killed while defending a settlement of rabbit families; Zerakh helped keep the coalition from breaking. The family’s service reached beyond its own door. These stories place Inara inside the Three Years, not safely behind them as an adult remembering somebody else’s struggle.

Formal appeals accumulated while committees delayed and corporate influence softened distant urgency. The decisive catastrophe came when mercenaries destroyed multiple space elevators and widened the violence through orbital sabotage and redirected impacts. Falling lines, tsunami, debris and secondary failures turned the attack on a culture into a planetary disaster. The lore gives the destruction an appalling scale, while the songs return us to the people who had to stand beneath it.

The ones who came from above

Three Prefects descended toward Inara’s threatened home on near-invisible lines from high-altitude balloon lifts. They covered one another in a triangle and opened fire before landing. The advancing hyena squad was finished before the first boots met the ground. Their leader raised her visor: a white wolfess, blue-marked, familiar enough for Zyara and Inara to recognize the southern lineage. Lazaan Tal’rashaan had arrived.

The force that followed her report seized or destroyed stealth ships, exposed outer-belt bases, broke mercenary logistics, detained commanders and preserved evidence. What political delay had allowed to fester for three Tal’Shyran years was broken by concentrated intervention in three days. The contrast is the point: justice had not been impossible. It had been withheld.

Three days to leave

Lazaan returned with Kael’Rath alive, his lost arm replaced by a carefully fur-matched prosthetic. She offered of-age Inara a place at the academy. The choice remained Inara’s, with her parents’ permission; the commitment meant study, training, separation and a minimum eight Tal’Shyran years, equivalent to ten galactic-standard years, of service. These figures measure a service term, not Inara’s age or the date of the story.

Inara chose to become the help that arrives for somebody else’s cubs. Family blessings followed, carrying Tal’Jahhran, strength, justice and safe passage beyond Babylon harm. Lazaan would return in three days. The future Starfall left neither because her home had become worthless nor because the stars promised a cleaner life. Forest law had grown large enough to follow her off-world.
